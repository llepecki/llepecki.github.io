# M2 moderation kit — child comprehension sessions

Companion to `implementation-review-2026-08-31.md`, finding M2. A moderator
runs short observation sessions with children aged 7–10 and records outcomes
below. Nothing in these sessions is stored in the app and no analytics exist;
the record sheet is the only artifact.

## Participants and consent

- At least three children total, covering both the 7–8 and 9–10 bands.
- At least one English and one Polish session when both audiences are
  available.
- A guardian consents beforehand; the child assents at the start and may stop
  at any time. Record no names, recordings, or identifiers — participant
  codes (P1, P2, …) with age band and session language only.
- One child per session, 15–25 minutes, on a device the child can operate
  comfortably (mouse or touch). Start from a fresh browser profile so the
  Guide opens on its own.

## Moderator rules

- Use neutral prompts only: "What do you think this does?", "What would you
  try next?", "Can you tell me what happened?" (PL: "Jak myślisz, co to
  robi?", "Czego spróbujesz teraz?", "Powiesz mi, co się stało?").
- Never name a control, point at the screen, or teach the answer during a
  task. If the child is stuck for more than about a minute, note the
  blockage, then help and mark the task "assisted".
- Stop the session if the child is frustrated or asks to stop; a stopped
  session still counts for whatever was observed.

## Tasks

1. **Guide.** "This app has a little tour. Go through it and do what it asks."
   Observe: does the child find Next, answer step 2's turning question, work
   the step 5 cards, place and check the step 8 force, and choose a final
   action — all without being told which control to use?
2. **Turning-effect concept.** Ask afterwards: "What changes how strongly a
   push turns the beam?" (PL: "Od czego zależy, jak mocno siła obraca
   belkę?"). Listen for BOTH force strength and distance from the pivot (any
   child wording counts: "how hard you push" / "how far from the middle").
3. **Pivot and perpendicular distance.** Back on Guide step 5 (reopen it with
   the "Guide" button under the mode selector), ask the child to point out the pivot and to
   show which distance matters for card B. Listen for the shortest/straight
   distance to the line, not the distance along the beam.
4. **One Balance and one Predict mission.** "Try this game and make the beam
   balance." then "Now try the other game and guess which way it turns."
   Observe: finds Start, places the force, uses Check, reads the feedback,
   continues after a wrong answer without distress; in Predict, chooses an
   answer deliberately (ask "why that one?").
5. **Free Explore.** "Change the force however you like and tell me what
   happens." Observe whether the child connects their change (strength,
   position, or direction) to the moment bars, the arc, or the pill result.

## Pass rubric

The finding passes when, across participants:

- every child finds the primary action of each surface unaided (Guide Next,
  game Start, Check balance, a Predict answer);
- explanations of the turning effect use force strength AND distance, not
  strength alone;
- no child equates the moment with "speed", "energy", or plain "force wins";
  probe with: "Is the strongest push always best at turning? Why?" (PL: "Czy
  najsilniejsze pchnięcie zawsze obraca najlepiej? Dlaczego?");
- interface chrome (language button, panel labels, HUD) does not become the
  main object of attention during play.

Treat any repeated navigation blockage, misleading term, or chrome
distraction as a design finding. If the same problem appears for two
participants, stop further sessions, revise the wording or design, re-run the
automated gates, and repeat the affected task with new participants.
